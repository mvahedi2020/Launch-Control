# Product risks: launch judgment

This is a prospective product-risk register for a fictional sample, not a report of incidents.

| Risk | Signal to observe | Response before further investment |
|---|---|---|
| A high readiness percentage substitutes for required gates | A participant says a launch should proceed despite a listed blocker. | Test the blocker explanation and the distinction between summary and permission. Preserve the required-gate rule. |
| Evidence text is mistaken for verified evidence | A reviewer assumes the app validated a URL, rehearsal, or claim. | Explain that current validation checks presence, not truth. A real workflow would need provenance and accountable review. |
| An old approval is treated as permanent | A participant expects Go to survive removal of required evidence. | Observe the withdrawal event and require a fresh decision in the scenario; reconsider wording if the transition is missed. |
| Simulation is mistaken for a deployment | A participant expects a button to change a real service. | Treat misunderstanding as a stop signal in the study. Keep all actions local and explicitly simulated. |
| A local timeline is treated as a compliance audit log | A participant assumes identity verification or tamper resistance. | State that browser storage is an inspection aid. Shared audit controls require a separately designed identity and retention policy. |
| The two response options oversimplify incident management | A reviewer assumes every incident should pause or roll back. | Bound the exercise to its fictional scenario. Do not use it as an operational incident procedure. |

After formative sessions, choose one repeated misunderstanding to address before expanding scope. Risk reduction in a prototype is not proof of production reliability or compliance.
