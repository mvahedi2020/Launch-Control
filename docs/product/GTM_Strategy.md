# Go-to-market hypotheses

## User, sponsor, and problem

The proposed primary user is a B2B SaaS launch lead assembling readiness from several functions. Evidence contributors need to understand which prerequisite they own; a potential budget sponsor would be the product operations or release-function leader accountable for a repeatable review process. These roles and buying assumptions require discovery and are not validated segments.

## Positioning and alternatives

The positioning hypothesis is an inspectable readiness conversation: who owns the prerequisite, what evidence is supplied, and why a decision changed. Alternatives include an existing project tracker, a checklist in a shared document, or a facilitated readiness meeting. The product must demonstrate a reason to switch; displaying a new dashboard alone is insufficient.

## Proposed sequence

1. Conduct five formative fictional-data sessions and resolve recurring misunderstanding of gates, evidence, or simulation.
2. Run the proposed 20-participant study only after the task protocol is stable. Use the PRD's 18/20 explanation and 17/20 unassisted-completion targets as decision inputs.
3. If comprehension and repeated problem evidence support further work, design a consented, bounded pilot with a sponsor and an agreed data policy. The current prototype does not accept real launch information for such a pilot.

A later pilot could compare time and errors in reconstructing a decision with the team's existing method. Collect a baseline first; no time-saving percentage, demand, revenue, pricing, or adoption result is claimed.

## Investment gates

A real product would need evidence provenance, identity, permissions, retention, support ownership, and privacy/security review. Keep deployment, paging, and traffic control outside the scope until separately authorized. Stop the commercial path if users understand the sample but prefer their existing process or cannot identify a recurring problem worth funding.
